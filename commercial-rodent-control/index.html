Why commercial accounts need a different approach.

A restaurant, retail shop or office deals with different pressures than a single-family home, regular deliveries, dumpster areas, shared walls with neighboring units, and health code requirements that a homeowner never has to think about. Businesses along the Blanding Boulevard and Park Avenue retail corridors see steady delivery and foot traffic that can draw activity if food storage or waste areas are not managed carefully.

An inspection for a commercial account covers the dumpster and loading area, storage rooms, kitchen equipment where applicable, and shared walls or utility chases that can let activity travel between adjoining units in a strip center.

Monitoring and documentation for compliance.

Many commercial accounts need ongoing monitoring rather than a single visit, both to catch new activity early and to maintain documentation for health inspections or landlord requirements. We provide clear written records of what was checked, what was found and what was done on each visit, not a verbal summary that is hard to produce later if needed.

  • Dumpster and loading areas checked for activity and food sources.
  • Shared walls and utility chases checked for cross-unit entry points.
  • Written documentation provided for health inspection or landlord records.
A verbal summary does not help when a health inspector asks for records.

Scheduling around business hours.

Commercial visits are scheduled around operating hours where possible, before opening, after closing, or during a slow period, so the inspection and any treatment does not disrupt customers or staff. For a restaurant kitchen specifically, we coordinate placement to stay clear of food prep and service areas.

Ongoing accounts versus one-time visits.

Some businesses need a single visit to resolve a specific problem; others benefit from a recurring monitoring schedule, particularly food service businesses with ongoing delivery and waste exposure. We discuss which makes sense for your specific business rather than defaulting to a recurring contract that is not actually needed.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.
