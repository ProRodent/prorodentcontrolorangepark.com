Garages carry most of the lower-level pressure here.

Most Orange Park homes are built on a slab without a true basement, so the garage, utility room and under-house crawl space carry most of the lower-level rodent pressure a basement would handle in other parts of the country. Garage door gaps and seals that have worn down over years of use are some of the most common entry points we find.

Stored boxes, holiday decorations and yard equipment packed into a garage for long stretches without being checked are exactly the kind of undisturbed material mice use for nesting. A garage that gets opened only to park the car, without anyone moving items around regularly, can host activity for a while before anyone notices.

What a garage inspection checks.

A technician checks the garage door seal and threshold, any gaps around the service door, utility penetrations for water, electrical or gas lines, and the area where the garage meets the main house framing. Stored items get moved or checked where droppings or gnaw marks suggest activity, rather than leaving boxes stacked against a wall unexamined.

  • Garage door seals and thresholds checked for gaps.
  • Utility penetrations where lines enter the garage traced for openings.
  • Stored boxes and equipment checked for droppings or gnawing.
A garage door seal worn down over years is an easy way in that nobody notices until something is already inside.

Protecting wiring and stored belongings.

Garages often hold a water heater, HVAC equipment or electrical panels with exposed wiring that rodents will chew given the chance. Checking and protecting these specifically, not just looking for droppings on the floor, is part of a thorough garage visit, and any damage found gets documented honestly rather than glossed over.

After the visit.

Once entry points are sealed and active rodents cleared, we advise on simple habits that keep the garage less inviting going forward. That might mean storing items in sealed bins rather than cardboard boxes, for example, without turning the conversation into an upsell for products you do not need.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about garage and basement rodent control

Why do garages get so much rodent activity here?

Most Orange Park homes do not have a true basement, so the garage and utility areas carry the lower-level pressure a basement would handle elsewhere, and worn door seals are a common entry point.

Can rodents damage a garage's electrical or utility lines?

Yes, exposed wiring and utility penetrations are checked specifically since rodents will chew wiring given access.

Should I store things in plastic bins instead of boxes?

It helps. Sealed bins are far less inviting for nesting than cardboard, though the bigger fix is still closing the actual entry point.
