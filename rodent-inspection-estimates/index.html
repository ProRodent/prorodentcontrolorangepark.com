What the inspection covers.

An inspection is not a quick look around. A technician walks the exterior perimeter checking the roofline, soffits, foundation vents and utility penetrations, then moves inside to check the attic, garage, kitchen and any area where droppings, gnaw marks or odor have been reported. Both mouse and Norway rat activity get checked for, since the two often leave similar early signs.

Addresses inside our Orange Park coverage ZIPs, 32065 and 32073, are scheduled directly. Mention the age of the house and any recent roof or siding work when you call, since older homes and newer construction tend to show different entry points.

How the estimate works.

The price quoted after an inspection reflects what was actually found, not a number given sight unseen over the phone. If the visit finds a single entry point and light activity, the plan and price reflect that. If it finds multiple gaps, active nesting and some chewed insulation, the estimate reflects that instead, broken into clear line items rather than one vague total.

  • Every entry point found gets listed, not just the worst one.
  • Damage, if any, is priced separately and explained.
  • The price is confirmed before any treatment work begins.
An honest estimate starts with an honest look at the property, not a guess from the driveway.

What happens if nothing is found.

Occasionally an inspection turns up no active rodent evidence at all, just a sound that turned out to be something else, or activity that had already stopped. We say so plainly rather than recommending treatment that is not actually needed. A property with no entry points open and no active signs does not need sealing it does not have a reason for.

Scheduling an inspection.

Same-day inspections are offered where the schedule allows, and the line is answered 24 hours a day. Whether the concern is urgent, a loud scratching sound overnight, or routine, wanting a check before closing on a home purchase, the same thorough walk-through applies either way.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.
