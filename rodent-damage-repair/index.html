What rodent damage actually looks like.

Chewed insulation is one of the most common forms of damage we find, since mice and rats both use insulation as nesting material and gnaw through it indiscriminately in the process. Ductwork, especially flexible duct runs in an attic, can develop holes that let conditioned air escape into the attic rather than the living space, which shows up as a utility bill creeping up before anyone finds the physical damage.

Soffits and roofline trim on older homes sometimes show gnawed openings around vents that have widened over time, and drywall occasionally needs repair where an entry point was chewed through from the attic side down into a wall cavity.

How damage gets documented.

Every piece of damage found, whether during an inspection, a cleanup or a trapping visit, gets photographed and priced as its own line item rather than folded quietly into a larger total. You see exactly what was found, where, and why it costs what it costs, before any repair work begins.

  • Insulation, duct, soffit and drywall damage priced separately and explained.
  • Photos provided so you can see what was found before agreeing to repair.
  • Repairs scheduled only after the entry point itself has been sealed.
Damage hidden in a larger total is damage you cannot evaluate honestly.

Why repair follows sealing, not the other way around.

Repairing chewed insulation or ductwork before rodent exclusion and entry point sealing closes the entry point means risking the same damage again within weeks. We confirm the entry point is sealed first, then schedule repair work, so the fix actually holds instead of needing to be redone.

What repair involves.

Insulation is replaced in the affected area rather than patched over damaged material, ductwork is repaired or replaced depending on the extent of the damage, and soffit or drywall repairs are matched to the existing material where reasonably possible. The goal is a repair that actually resolves the damage, not a cosmetic patch.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Will repair be priced as part of a larger total?

No. Damage is documented and priced as its own line item so you know exactly what you are paying for.

Do you repair before or after sealing the entry point?

After. Repairing before the gap is closed risks the same damage happening again, so sealing comes first.

Can chewed ductwork really affect my utility bill?

Yes, a hole in a duct run in the attic can let conditioned air escape, which shows up as higher bills before the physical damage is found.
