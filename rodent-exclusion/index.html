Finding every gap before closing any of them.

Sealing only works if every entry point gets found first. A single missed gap leaves the whole job vulnerable, since rodents will use whichever opening is still available regardless of how well the others were closed. The exterior perimeter gets checked methodically: roofline and soffits, foundation vents, utility penetrations and garage door seals.

Older homes often have gaps around aging roof vents and low masonry foundations that have settled over the decades. Newer construction tends to have tighter, more specific utility penetrations instead, a single gap around a pipe or conduit rather than widespread wear.

What materials actually hold.

Steel wool, hardware cloth and sheet metal are used depending on the size and shape of the gap, since rodents can chew through caulk, soft foam and even some plastics within days. A foundation vent gets a properly fitted mesh patch, not a smear of sealant that looks finished but gives a mouse nothing to resist.

  • Hardware cloth and steel mesh sized to the specific gap.
  • Mortar repair for masonry foundation joints where needed.
  • Garage door sweeps and thresholds checked and replaced where gaps exist.
Caulk looks finished. A rodent does not care how it looks.

Sealing after active removal.

If rodents are already active on the property, rodent trapping and removal happens first, since sealing an occupied space just traps the problem inside rather than keeping it out. Once the inspection confirms the property is clear, sealing proceeds across every gap found.

Confirming the seal holds.

We check back after the work is done to confirm no new gnaw marks or droppings have appeared near a sealed point. If a seal does not hold, that is worth knowing honestly rather than assuming the job finished cleanly, and we address it directly on the follow-up visit.

Questions about rodent exclusion and entry point sealing

Why not just use caulk?

Caulk and soft foam can be chewed through within days. Steel mesh, hardware cloth and properly fitted sheet metal hold up to repeated attempts in a way caulk does not.

Do you seal before or after removing active rodents?

After. Sealing an occupied space traps the problem inside instead of keeping new activity out, so removal comes first.

How do you know you found every gap?

A full perimeter walk-through checks the roofline, foundation, vents and utility penetrations systematically rather than stopping at the first gap found.
