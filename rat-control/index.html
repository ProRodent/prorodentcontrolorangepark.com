Why rat calls differ from mouse calls.

Norway rats are bigger, bolder and leave different evidence than house mice. Droppings are noticeably larger, gnaw marks are wider, and the sounds are heavier, more of a deliberate scratching or thump than the quick skittering a mouse makes. An inspection confirms which animal is actually involved before any treatment plan gets written.

In Orange Park, rat activity tends to concentrate near Doctors Lake and the St. Johns River frontage, where mature tree cover, irrigation and water access give Norway rats everything they need close together. Yards backing onto the water see more outdoor burrow activity along the fence line than interior lots further from either waterway.

Tracing the entry point.

Rats need a gap of about half an inch to get inside, roughly the size of a quarter. On local homes that usually means a roofline gap near a soffit return, a foundation vent that has lost its screen, or a utility penetration under the house that was never properly closed. We check the exterior perimeter methodically rather than guessing from where droppings happen to be found inside.

  • Roofline, soffit and vent gaps checked first.
  • Foundation and crawl space penetrations traced along the perimeter.
  • Yard-side burrow activity assessed where the lot backs onto water or dense tree cover.
A rat problem rarely starts indoors. It starts at the gap that let it get there.

Trapping and sealing together.

Trapping clears the rats already active on the property, using placements matched to where droppings, rub marks and gnawing actually show up, not a generic station count. Sealing closes the gap that let them in with steel mesh, hardware cloth or sheet metal, since caulk alone gives a determined rat nothing to resist chewing through.

For a yard with active burrow activity near the water, backyard and outdoor rodent control often runs alongside the trapping and sealing work on the house itself, since treating only the house leaves the yard-side source untouched.

Follow-up and what to expect.

Signs the sealing is holding.

  • No new gnaw marks near the sealed entry point.
  • No fresh droppings in previously active areas.
  • Traps staying empty on the return visit.

We return to check the traps and confirm the seal is holding, and we explain honestly if a second entry point turns up that was missed on the first pass, which happens occasionally on older, larger lots with more than one vulnerable spot.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rat control

Are Norway rats common in Orange Park?

Yes, particularly on properties near Doctors Lake and the St. Johns River where water, tree cover and landscaping give rats what they need close together.

How is rat control different from mouse control?

The entry points are larger and the inspection focuses more on the roofline, foundation perimeter and yard, since Norway rats travel and burrow differently than house mice.

Can rat problems come from the yard without an indoor sighting?

Yes. Outdoor burrow activity along a fence line or woodpile is common on waterfront lots even before any sign shows up inside the house.
