What the visit involves.

A mouse call almost always starts with a sound, scratching behind a cabinet, something moving inside a wall after dark, or droppings turning up in a kitchen drawer. The first thing a technician does on an Orange Park visit is confirm it is actually a house mouse and not a young Norway rat, since the likely entry points and the treatment plan differ for each.

House mice are small enough to pass through gaps about a quarter inch wide. On an Orange Park house that usually means a gap under a garage door, a crack in an older block foundation, or a plumbing penetration under a sink or washing machine that was never properly sealed. We walk the exterior first, checking these specific points before going inside.

Inside, we check the sill plate, baseboards in the kitchen and laundry area, and any cabinet with plumbing running through it. Droppings, grease marks and gnawed packaging tell us where activity is heaviest, and that is where traps go first.

How we remove mice.

Snap traps and enclosed bait stations are placed along the routes we actually find, not scattered through the house at random. Mice travel the same paths repeatedly, hugging walls and cabinet edges, so a trap placed off that path rarely catches anything.

  • Traps placed along confirmed travel routes, not guessed locations.
  • Bait stations used only where children and pets cannot reach them.
  • A second visit to clear traps and check for new activity.
Removal without sealing only resets the clock.

Keeping them out.

Once the mice inside are cleared, entry points get sealed with steel wool, hardware cloth or sheet metal depending on the gap, materials mice cannot chew through the way they can chew drywall or soft foam. A cracked foundation vent gets a proper mesh patch rather than a smear of caulk a mouse can push through within a week.

Garage door gaps and sill plate cracks are the most common entry points we find on Orange Park homes, especially older houses where the slab foundation has settled or shifted slightly over the decades. Addressing those specific points is often the single most effective fix on the whole job.

After the visit.

What to watch for after treatment.

  • No new droppings in the same spot for at least a week.
  • No scratching sounds at night in the same wall or ceiling area.
  • Traps staying empty on the follow-up visit.

If activity continues after sealing, that usually means a second entry point was missed, not that the first fix failed. The follow-up visit is built around checking for exactly that, and we explain what we are checking before we leave.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about mouse control

How can I tell if I have mice or rats?

Mouse droppings are small, about the size of a grain of rice, and mice tend to stay low, inside cabinets and along baseboards. Norway rats are louder and more often heard at ground level.

How do mice get into homes in Orange Park?

Garage door gaps, cracked foundation vents and unsealed plumbing penetrations under sinks are the most common entry points we find on local houses.

Will traps alone solve the problem?

Trapping clears what is already inside, but sealing the entry points is what stops new mice from replacing them.

Is treatment safe around children and pets?

Tell the technician about every child and pet in the home. Bait stations are placed only where they cannot reach them, and we explain exactly what was used and where.

Where mouse calls come from around Orange Park

Mouse calls come in from the older ranch-style homes near the river almost as often as they do from newer construction further from the water. Tell us the street and where you hear the scratching and we plan the visit from there.
