Placement comes from the inspection, not a guess.

Trapping only works when the placements match where the animal is actually traveling. A technician places traps along confirmed routes found during the inspection, walls, cabinet edges, foundation lines and garage corners, rather than setting a fixed number of traps and hoping some of them land in the right spot.

On Orange Park properties near Doctors Lake or the St. Johns River, outdoor trapping along a fence line or near a woodpile is common for Norway rats, while indoor mouse trapping concentrates on kitchens, laundry areas and garages regardless of which part of town the call comes from.

Snap traps versus bait stations.

Snap traps are used where activity is confirmed and children or pets cannot reach them. Enclosed bait stations are used in areas that need a less visible option, along an exterior foundation line or inside a garage with limited household access. The choice depends on the specific location, not a single default method applied everywhere.

  • Traps checked and cleared on a set schedule, not left indefinitely.
  • Placement reviewed and adjusted if the first round shows no activity.
  • Bait stations kept out of reach of children and pets.
A trap in the wrong spot catches nothing. The inspection is what makes the placement right.

Why trapping alone is not the end of the job.

Removing the animals already inside does not stop new ones from arriving through the same gap. Trapping and removal is almost always paired with rodent exclusion and entry point sealing once the active population has been cleared, so the fix actually holds instead of resetting within a few weeks.

The follow-up visit.

We return to clear traps, check for new activity, and confirm whether the problem has resolved or whether a second entry point needs attention. Clean traps on the return visit are a good sign; continued activity after the first round usually points to a gap that was missed, which we check for directly rather than repeating the same placement.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent trapping and removal

How long before traps catch anything?

It varies by how active the population is, but most properties show results within the first few days once placement matches the actual travel routes.

Are the traps safe around pets?

Snap traps go only where pets cannot reach, and bait stations are enclosed and placed away from pet access points.

Will trapping alone fix the problem long term?

Usually not by itself. Pairing trapping with sealing the entry point is what keeps new rodents from replacing the ones removed.
